---
title: Thunderstorms and mudflows in the mountains
summary: In summer the weather in the Almaty mountains changes within an hour. How to spot the danger early and what to do in a thunderstorm or a mudflow.
---
In the mountains above Almaty a summer day often goes like this: a clear morning, thunderstorms and downpours in the afternoon. Heavy rain, or heat that melts mountain ice fast, can trigger a mudflow — a surge of water, rocks and mud down a riverbed.

## Before you set out

- Check the forecast and the warnings of the Emergencies Ministry and Kazselezashchita (the mudflow protection service). If there is a storm warning, do not go into the mountains.
- Start early and be back by midday: thunderstorms usually begin in the afternoon.
- Tell someone where you are going and when you will be back.

## Thunderstorm

Count the seconds between the flash and the thunder. Under 30 means the storm is close — time to take shelter.

- Get off ridges, summits and open slopes. Do not stand under a lone tree or by the water — get out of the river and the boat.
- A car with the windows closed or a building is good shelter. A tent does not protect from lightning.
- If there is no shelter, crouch on a mat or your backpack, feet together, head down. Do not lie on the ground.
- In a group, do not huddle: stay several metres apart.
- Carry on 30 minutes after the last thunder.

## Mudflow

Signs: a growing roar and rumble up the gorge; the river suddenly turns muddy, rises or, on the contrary, suddenly drops away.

1. Climb up the slope at once, **across** the riverbed, not along it. You have minutes.
2. Leave your things — do not try to save the car or the tent.
3. Do not cross the river or go back into the riverbed until the flow has passed and the water is back to normal.

> Do not camp in the riverbed or on a low terrace of a mountain river. Emergency — **112**.
