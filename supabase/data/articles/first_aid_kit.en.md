---
title: A first aid kit for fishing and hiking
summary: What to pack in a first aid kit for a day or a weekend by the water or in the mountains, and how to keep it.
---
Out in nature a doctor is often an hour or more away, and there is not always a signal. A small first aid kit solves most minor troubles and helps you hold on until help arrives with serious ones.

## Wounds and cuts

- Antiseptic: chlorhexidine or hydrogen peroxide.
- Sterile bandages and wipes, plasters of different sizes, a roll of adhesive tape.
- An elastic bandage — for sprains and to hold a splint.
- Scissors and tweezers — to remove a splinter, a tick or a piece of a hook.

## Medicines

- A painkiller and fever reducer (for example, paracetamol or ibuprofen).
- An antihistamine — for wasp and bee stings and pollen.
- A sorbent (activated charcoal or similar) and oral rehydration salts — for food poisoning and in the heat.
- Any medicines you take regularly, with a day's spare.

## For the outdoors

- Mosquito and tick repellent.
- Sunscreen and lip balm.
- An emergency (space) blanket: it weighs 50 grams and keeps you warm if you get chilled.
- A burn ointment or gel — by the campfire and the grill.

## How to keep it

1. Pack everything in a waterproof bag or a brightly coloured container — it is easy to find.
2. Once a season check the expiry dates and replace what was used.
3. Tell your companions where the kit is.

**A hook in the skin:** if it went in shallow and has no barb, pull it out and apply antiseptic. If it is deep, in the face or near the eye, do not pull: tape the hook in place and go to a doctor.

> This article does not replace a doctor. Before the trip, ask a doctor which medicines are right for you. Emergency — **112**.
