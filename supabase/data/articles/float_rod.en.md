---
title: The float rod: setting up and tuning
summary: The simplest rig that almost everyone starts with. What to buy, how to put it together and how to tune the float so you see every bite.
---
A float rod is good for crucian carp, roach, perch, bream and carp close to the bank. It is cheap, and you can catch fish with it from day one.

## What you need

- A **rod** 4–6 metres long — a pole without rings, or a rod with rings and a reel.
- **Line** 0.16–0.2 mm for roach and crucian carp, 0.25–0.3 mm if you expect carp.
- A **float** carrying 1–4 grams: the further out and deeper you fish and the stronger the wind, the heavier it should be.
- **Split shot** to match the float's load.
- **Hooks** size 6–10: the higher the number, the smaller the hook. Small ones for maggots, bigger ones for worms and corn.

## How to put it together

1. Tie the line to the pole or spool it on the reel.
2. Thread the float on — through its eye or with silicone sleeves.
3. Pinch the shot onto the line below the float: most of it close to the float, one small shot 15–20 cm from the hook.
4. Tie on the hook with a Palomar or clinch knot (see the article on three knots).

## How to tune it

- **Shotting.** Add shot until only the tip of the float's antenna is above the water. An under-shotted float shows bites late; an over-shotted one sinks.
- **Depth.** Clip a plummet or a depth clip on the hook and lower the rig. Move the float until its antenna stands just above the water. Now the bait is at the bottom. For crucian carp and bream, move the float up another 5–10 cm so the bait lies on the bottom.

## Baits

Worms, maggots, sweetcorn, dough, pearl barley. Try different ones: fish often prefer one.

> Strike with a short flick of the wrist when the float goes under or moves off decisively.
