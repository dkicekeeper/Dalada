---
title: A clean shore
summary: Simple rules so that a place is as good after you as it was before — rubbish, fishing line, campfires and toilets.
---
Good places are not spoiled by anglers but by what they leave behind. If everyone takes their own things away, the shore stays a place you want to come back to.

## Rubbish

- Bring bags and take away everything you brought: cans, bags, cigarette ends, food scraps.
- Do not bury or burn rubbish: animals dig up pits, and plastic and foil do not burn in a fire.
- Glass on the shore means cuts for people and animals. Better not to bring it at all.
- If you can, pick up someone else's bag too. The place gets clean faster that way.

## Line and tackle

Discarded fishing line lies in the grass and water for years. Birds, hedgehogs and fish get tangled in it.

- Collect bits of line, hooks and sinkers in your pocket or a box and take them away.
- If your line snags in a tree, try to get it down rather than leave it hanging.

## Campfire

1. Light your fire in an existing fire ring, not in a new spot.
2. Do not cut living trees — gather dead wood.
3. When you leave, pour water on the fire and check by hand that the embers are cold.
4. In dry weather and strong wind, no fire: a single spark can set the steppe and reeds alight.

## Toilet and washing

- Go at least 60 metres from the water, dig a hole 15–20 cm deep and fill it in. Take the paper with you in a bag.
- Wash dishes away from the shore and without detergent; pour dirty water on the ground, not into the water.

> National parks have their own rules: the park decides where you may camp and light a fire. Boundaries are on the Dalada map, layer National parks and reserves.
