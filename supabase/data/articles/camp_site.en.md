---
title: Where to pitch your tent
summary: How to choose a campsite by the water and in the mountains so that you are not flooded, blown away or hit by something from above at night.
---
Look for a good campsite while it is still light: in the dark you cannot see the slope, a dry riverbed or dead branches overhead.

## What to avoid

- **Riverbeds and floodplains.** Do not pitch in a dry riverbed, on a gravel bar or right at the water. In the mountains water rises within minutes after a downpour, and the Almaty region has mudflows in summer.
- **Dead trees and branches.** Look up: dead trees and broken branches fall even in a light wind.
- **Hollows.** Cold air and rainwater collect in a dip. A little higher is warmer and drier.
- **The foot of scree slopes and cliffs.** Rocks fall without warning.
- **Anthills and animal trails.**

## What to look for

- A flat spot the size of your tent, free of stones and roots.
- Shelter from the wind — bushes, a mound, a big rock. At Kapshagay and Balkhash the wind can be stronger at night than in the day — pitch with the entrance away from the wind.
- Water nearby, but not right next to you: 30–50 metres from the shore is enough.
- A spot where someone has camped before: less damage to the grass.

## Before you sleep

1. Tighten all the guy lines, even if the evening is calm.
2. Put food in closed containers or in the car: dogs, foxes and rodents come at night.
3. Put the fire out with water and stir the embers: they must be cold to the touch.

> In national parks tents may only be pitched in designated places. Park boundaries are on the Dalada map, layer National parks and reserves.
